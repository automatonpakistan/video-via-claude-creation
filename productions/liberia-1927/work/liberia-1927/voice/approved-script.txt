Fifteen thousand registered voters. Two hundred and forty-three thousand votes for one man.

That's the record from Liberia's nineteen twenty-seven presidential election.

The man was President Charles D. B. King, running for another term against Thomas Faulkner.

King was credited with more than sixteen times as many votes as there were registered voters in the entire country.

Even if every registered voter had chosen him, he'd still have been two hundred and twenty-eight thousand votes short.

And Faulkner? He was credited with another nine thousand.

Put those totals together, and the reported votes amount to almost seventeen ballots for every registered voter.

The election later entered the Guinness Book of Records as the most fraudulent ever reported.

King got another term. And the election got a world record.
