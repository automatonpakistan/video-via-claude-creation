## Sources and fact check

- Africanews, “Elections history in Africa's oldest democratic republic: Liberia”: https://www.africanews.com/2017/10/08/elections-history-in-africa-s-oldest-democratic-republic-liberia/ — reports about 15,000 registered voters, 243,000 votes for Charles D. B. King, 9,000 for Thomas J. Faulkner, and inclusion in the Guinness Book of Records.
- United States Institute of Peace, Peaceworks 143 (2018), “What Works in Preventing Election Violence: Evidence from Liberia and Kenya”: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-Y3_P31-PURL-gpo147734/pdf/GOVPUB-Y3_P31-PURL-gpo147734.pdf — reports 235,000 votes for King and 15,000 eligible voters, and the historical Guinness listing. This demonstrates that published totals differ.
- The user selected the 243,000/15,000 account and requested “the record from Liberia” in narration instead of “the widely reported tally.” Published totals differ, as documented above. A current Guinness record page was not located; describe a historical listing rather than an independently verified current record.
- Arithmetic for that account: 243,000 / 15,000 = 16.2; 243,000 - 15,000 = 228,000; (243,000 + 9,000) / 15,000 = 16.8. These calculations describe the reported tally, not legitimate turnout.

## Narration draft

Fifteen thousand registered voters. Two hundred and forty-three thousand votes for one man.

That's the record from Liberia's nineteen twenty-seven presidential election.

The man was President Charles D. B. King, running for another term against Thomas Faulkner.

King was credited with more than sixteen times as many votes as there were registered voters in the entire country.

Even if every registered voter had chosen him, he'd still have been two hundred and twenty-eight thousand votes short.

And Faulkner? He was credited with another nine thousand.

Put those totals together, and the reported votes amount to almost seventeen ballots for every registered voter.

The election later entered the Guinness Book of Records as the most fraudulent ever reported.

King got another term. And the election got a world record.
